# How ChimeraBoost is benchmarked

Five suites, each with one job. Only one of them decides whether a change ships.

| suite | what it is | job | blocks a ship? |
|---|---|---|---|
| SynthGen | prior-sampled synthetic generators | mechanism screen: does the idea do anything at all? | no |
| **Grinsztajn + high-card** | 59 + 22 real datasets | **the decision**, sign-tested per stratum | **yes** |
| PMLB | 25 curated sets, tune/holdout folds | hyperparameter tuning only | no |
| **Public** | 22 audited datasets, ≥50k rows | post-hoc validation and published evidence | no |
| **TabArena** | the community leaderboard | the sealed out-of-sample read | no, report-only |

The separation is the point. A suite you tune against cannot also tell you whether you
have overfit it.

## The published chart

![strength vs speed](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/bbstats/chimeraboost/main/images/public_pareto.png)

Generated by `benchmarks/make_public_pareto.py` from a `--public` run.

**Axes.** Vertical is average rank across the suite, 1 = best, plotted so up is better.
Horizontal is the median fit-time multiple against the fastest model on each dataset.

**Why the median and not the mean.** Slowdowns are ratios, and ratios are right-skewed
by construction: a model can be 900x slower but never 900x faster. On this suite one
dataset (`fars`, where CatBoost takes 2,883 s against LightGBM's 3 s, a 970x ratio)
roughly doubles CatBoost's mean on its own, taking it from 52x to 121x. Our own default
shows the same shape, at 7x median and 15x mean. The table prints both, because they
answer different questions: the median is the typical dataset, the mean is the
whole-suite bill.

**Both axes are competitor-relative.** Each ChimeraBoost `quality` rung is scored
against CatBoost and LightGBM only, never against a sibling rung. Rank the whole field
together and the stronger rung banks a free point every time it beats the weaker one,
which is us beating ourselves; it also means adding a rung silently moves every other
row. Here a rung's rank comes from its own three-way contest, so adding or dropping
rungs cannot move anybody else.

Rank and win rate are the same matchups summarised two ways, and they still differ
slightly on CatBoost, because a competitor's rank averages over the contests it appears
in, one of which includes a weaker rung. The defensible claim is the conservative one:
**within noise of CatBoost at under a third of its median fit time.**

## Weighting

A benchmark suite is skewed by accident of what exists. This one runs 9 binary and 9
multiclass datasets against only 4 regression, 11 medium-sized against 5 large, and 7
high-cardinality against 4 low. Averaging unweighted lets whichever stratum happened to
be over-collected vote twice.

So the aggregate **rakes three facets to equal mass**, across task, size and categorical
cardinality, using iterative proportional fitting, which matches all three marginals at
once. Weighting by the crossed cell instead would be wrong: 3 × 3 × 4 = 36 cells over 22
datasets leaves most of them empty, and a lone dataset in a rare cell would carry an
enormous weight.

Weights are capped at half to double the equal weight. Without the cap one dataset
reaches 3.17x and another 0.20x, which would let a single dataset decide the regression
column.

Balancing is not free. Effective sample size falls from 21 to 16.9, so error bars widen
about 11%. Every run prints the weight table, the balance achieved per facet, and the
effective sample size, and `--no-weights` reproduces the unweighted aggregate for
comparison.

**Balancing currently flatters us a little**, which is worth stating plainly: it moves
CatBoost's average rank from 1.81 to 1.92 and our default rung's from 2.00 to 1.97,
widening the gap between them, because the up-weighted regression datasets are where
CatBoost is weakest. The facets and their thresholds were fixed from the suite's own
structure before that was visible, and `--no-weights` reproduces the unweighted numbers
in one command.

## What the public suite is not

It is **not sealed** and it **never blocks a ship**. It is read freely and cited as
evidence. A surprise here is a signal to go looking on the decision suites, and is not
itself a verdict.

Datasets are still chosen by **data properties only**: row counts, cardinality,
missingness, task type. No benchmark result may influence which datasets are in the
suite, or it would be born cherry-picked.

Every candidate was audited for overlap against TabArena, Grinsztajn, PMLB, the
high-card suite, and a list of datasets already spent elsewhere. That includes aliases a
name match cannot catch, like `Winedata`, which is the wine-reviews data already in the
high-card suite. Selection also rejected datasets on evidence only the raw data holds: a
Google Play set where 46% of ratings are a zero sentinel, a cinema set where tickets ×
price equals the target exactly, and an `autos` upload that is a million-row generator
expansion of a 205-row classic.

## Variant families

`@sus25` and `@sus50` train on a quarter and a half of the training rows with the test
set unchanged, so a twin reads as a point on its parent's learning curve. `@time` orders
rows by a real timestamp and trains on the past.

**These stay out of the headline aggregate.** A twin is scored on exactly its parent's
test rows, making it a derived view rather than an independent dataset. Including one
would hand its parent's stratum a second vote invisibly, which is worse than the raw
skew the weighting exists to fix. They are reported as their own strata and never pooled
with their parents.

## Reproducing it

```
python benchmarks/run_benchmarks.py --public --seeds 3 --save \
    --models ChimeraBoost ChimeraBoostEns5 CatBoost LightGBM
python benchmarks/make_public_pareto.py benchmarks/results/<stamp>.json
```

The full audit trail, listing every dataset, why it was included, and every rejected
candidate with its reason, is in `benchmarks/PUBLIC_PLAN.md`.
